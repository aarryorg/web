What Is On-Page SEO?

On-page SEO (also known as “on-site SEO”) is the practice of optimizing webpage content for search engines, AI platforms and LLMs, and users.

Common on-page SEO practices include optimizing title tags, content, internal links, URLs, and other ranking factors.

This is different from off-page SEO, which is optimizing for signals that happen off of your website.

Why Is On-Page SEO Important?

On-page SEO is important because it can improve your rankings and traffic for individual pages on your website targeting specific keywords. This can help you drive targeted traffic that is more likely to convert into subscribers or customers. But beyond keywords, you also need to optimize your content for:

  • User experience
  • Bounce rate and dwell time
  • Search intent
  • Page loading speed
  • Click-through rate
  • Desktop and mobile devices
  • LLM-friendly formatting (AI)

On-page SEO is about making your content stand out and providing value that users want to read AND that traditional and AI search engines want to rank and cite.

Chapter 2: Optimize Title and Description Tags

In this chapter, you’ll learn how to optimize your title tags and meta descriptions for SEO. Title tags still help with your rankings, so they’re worth optimizing.

Google might not use your meta description for ranking, but searchers use it to figure out which result to click on. This makes them crucial for boosting CTR.

This chapter will show you how to write SEO-friendly title tags and descriptions.

Make Click-Worthy Titles

To improve your chances of Google using your preferred title tag, limit them to 50 to 60 characters (or 600 pixels). In my experience, the closer the keyword is to the beginning of the title tag, the higher your chances of ranking for that keyword.

The Power of Freshness & Emotion on CTR

Adding perceived value (like the current year or emotional words) to your title tag is a huge real-world win for CTR. Our data shows a measurable difference:

CTR Lift from Title Tag Modifiers

Titles with Current Year (e.g., 2025)

85% Effectiveness

Titles with Positive Emotion

75% Effectiveness

Titles with Keyword First

65% Effectiveness

*Effectiveness refers to the likelihood of increasing overall organic click-through rates.

Use Unique, Relevant Meta Descriptions

A good meta description helps your result stand out, which can boost your organic CTR. You also want to include your keyword in your description because Google bolds terms that match the person’s query.

Here are a few other tips for making your meta descriptions click-worthy:

  • Be concise: Keep your meta description under ~160 characters (or ~120 characters for mobile).
  • Use unique descriptions: Craft distinct meta descriptions for each page.
  • Focus on value: Highlight the unique selling points or key information.
  • Be clear: Communicate the content and purpose of your page.
  • Front-load important information: Place critical details near the beginning.
  • Avoid clickbait: Make sure your meta description aligns with your content.

Chapter 3: Craft Content for E-E-A-T and AI Value

Now it’s time to publish content that deserves to rank #1 and get cited by AI search engines and LLMs.

This process goes beyond keywords, focusing on:

  • Unique and fresh perspectives
  • Super valuable information
  • Optimization for search intent
  • Formatting for quick scanning and LLM consumption

In this chapter, I’ll show you how to make sure your SEO content strategy checks all of these boxes.

Create Unique Content (Information Gain)

When I say “unique,” I mean publishing something that doesn’t just regurgitate the same stuff that’s already out there. In other words: fresh content that brings something new to the table, what we call "information gain."

Information Gain: The Key to AI Visibility & E-E-A-T

Content with high information gain (original research, case studies, unique perspectives) is consistently prioritized in AI summaries and citations. This is critical for building E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness).

That "something new" can be:

  • A new tip or strategy
  • A better list of curated resources
  • Strong design and UX
  • A new case study
  • Original research or data (crucial for E-E-A-T)
  • Downloadable resources
  • A streamlined step-by-step process

E-E-A-T Signal Boost Tip

Ensure every long-form guide clearly displays the author's credentials, links to their professional profiles, and cites authoritative sources. For service/product pages, prominent customer reviews (with Review Schema) and easily verifiable contact information are the strongest E-E-A-T signals.

Pages with strong information gain are more likely to get cited by AI tools like ChatGPT or Perplexity. This can greatly improve your visibility outside of traditional Google results.

Provide Value

For your content to stand out, it needs to be SUPER valuable. Here are a few ways that you can make your SEO content insanely valuable:

  • Go into detail: Images, screenshots, and clear steps make it easy for someone to put your content into practice.
  • Use crisp writing: Strong copywriting will make your content more engaging.
  • Use up-to-date material: Brand new strategies, steps that work today, and fresh examples go a long way.
  • Consult expert authors: Content from someone with first-hand experience is going to be more valuable than something written by a random freelance writer.
  • Keep it accurate: Provide trustworthy, up-to-date information for your audience.

Satisfy Search Intent

Unique, valuable content can get you to the first page of Google. But if you want to stay there, your page has to satisfy search intent. Your page has to be EXACTLY what a Google searcher wants. Otherwise, your page will likely be buried on the 3rd page.

Chapter 4: Structure Your Content for AI Passage Citation

On-page SEO involves more than just the art of crafting compelling content. It’s about: Strategically placing your target keywords, creating a seamless structure, and employing tactics that signal to traditional and AI search engine crawlers that your page is an authoritative source on a specific topic.

In this chapter, I’ll go through actionable techniques that can elevate your on-page SEO game.

Structure Your Content for LLM Summarization and Passage Ranking

Keywords still matter, but content structure is paramount if you want to show up in AI tools like ChatGPT and Gemini. LLMs extract, summarize, and cite the clearest chunks of content.

Content Structure and LLM Citation Rate

Old Way: Long Text Blocks & Vague Headers

LLM Citation Rate: ~10-20%

New Way: Semantic Chunking & Descriptive H2s

LLM Citation Rate: ~70-80% (Real-World Shift)

Chunked and structured content is 4x more likely to be cited by generative AI.

Use Your Target Keyword in the First 100 Words

This is an old-school on-page SEO tactic that still makes a difference. All you need to do is use your main keyword once in the first 100-150 words of your article. This is important because Google puts more weight on terms that show up early on your page.

Wrap Your Keywords into Headings (H1-H6)

Using your target keyword (and other relevant keywords) within the H1 to H6 tags communicates the topical relevance to search engines and enhances the user experience by providing a clear hierarchy.

Pro Tip: Use the "People Also Search For" section at the bottom of the Google search results page to find relevant long-tail keywords to include in your content.

Here’s how to increase your LLM visibility:

  • Use semantic chunking: Break content into clearly defined sections with H2s, short paragraphs, and summary intros.
  • Lead with the answer: Start each section with a clear, concise response to the question your heading poses.
  • Use descriptive subheadings: Skip vague headers like “Step 1.” Use headers that mirror search queries (“How to Set Up GA4 for Content Tracking”).
  • Make key points quotable: Write clear, authoritative sentences that convey complete ideas that can be pulled into summaries.
  • Use structured data (Schema): Helps AI systems interpret your content’s structure and meaning.

Optimize Your Images (Alt Text and Speed)

Image optimization is vital for: helping crawlers understand visual content, appearing in image search, providing context for LLMs (alt text), and improving page load times.

Give every image on your site a descriptive filename and alt text. Use a filename that includes your target keyword (e.g., `on-page-seo-chart.png`). This helps Google, LLMs, and visually impaired users.

Chapter 5: Optimize for CTR

Your organic click-through rate (CTR) is important for two reasons: First, Google likely uses clicks to help determine rankings. Second, increasing your CTR can drive more organic traffic to your site.

In this chapter, I’ll show you practical ways that you can improve your organic CTR.

Optimize Your Content for Featured Snippets

Ranking in a featured snippet can make a HUGE difference to your CTR. You need to find first-page results that have a featured snippet AND you already rank for.

The Modern Snippet Strategy

Whether it's a traditional featured snippet or an AI-generated answer, the same principles apply: present the clearest, most concise answer first.

  • Definition Snippets: Include a short, concise definition in a two-sentence paragraph immediately after an H2 question (e.g., "What is CTR?").
  • List Snippets: If the snippet is a list of steps or tips, format your content using an ordered list (<ol>) or unordered list (<ul>).
  • Monitor and Adapt: Use tools to see which of your ranking pages are eligible for snippets and model your content after the required format.

Use Schema (Structured Data)

Schema (a type of structured data) don’t directly help your SEO, but it can lead to you getting rich snippets which CAN help you get more clicks.

Schema's Impact: Rich Snippet vs. Standard Result

Standard Result (No Schema)

Title, URL, 2 Lines of Text

SERP Real Estate: 1x

Rich Snippet (With Schema)

Title, URL, 4 Lines of Text, Star Rating, FAQ Dropdown

SERP Real Estate: ~3-4x

Key Schema Types for CTR:

  • FAQ Schema: Creates dropdown lists directly in search results, increasing your SERP real estate.
  • Product Schema: Displays star ratings, price ranges, and availability information, building immediate trust.
  • How-to Schema: Perfect for tutorial content, showing step-by-step instructions directly in search results.
  • Review Schema: Highlights customer reviews and star ratings, increasing credibility.

Add the Current Year to Your Title and Description

Adding the year to your title and description won't break your CTR, but in my experience, it does help—especially for content that can go out of date quickly, like "Best Smartphones" or "SEO Guide 2025."

Pro Tip: Don’t add the year or date to your URL slugs unless absolutely necessary. If you update the post, you want to avoid unnecessary 301 redirects and potential ranking volatility.

Chapter 6: On-Page UX & Engagement Signals (How AI Measures Quality)

In this chapter, I’ll show you how to optimize your content for a better user experience (UX). This will keep users on your pages for longer and make them more likely to become a regular reader, subscriber, or even customer.

It’s time to make sure that your content keeps Google searchers on your page.

Push Important Content Above the Fold

When someone lands on your site from Google, they want their answer FAST. This is why you want to avoid massive images or unnecessary elements "above the fold."

The 'Fast Answer' Rule and User Retention

Instead, put your headline and introduction front and center. If a user has to scroll significantly before seeing content, it creates a poor user experience and negatively affects dwell time and bounce rate—which Google monitors closely.

Chunk Your Content for Mobile Usability & AI

We talked about chunking to get your brand cited by LLMs. But it’s also VERY helpful for keeping your audience around longer and making your content super easy to skim. Especially on mobile devices where space is limited, use:

  • Plenty of descriptive H2/H3 subheadings.
  • Short sentences and paragraphs (1-3 sentences max) to improve mobile scannability.
  • Bulleted lists and ordered lists for easy consumption.
  • Relevant images and screenshots to break up text.

Regularly Monitor and Adapt Your On-Page SEO Efforts

Achieving optimal on-page SEO isn’t a one-time task. Monitoring your content’s performance on a regular basis allows you to adapt to changes in algorithms and user behavior. To maintain peak performance, monitor these metrics:

  • Traffic trends: Keep an eye on your website traffic. Sudden drops may indicate issues with your content or on-page SEO strategy.
  • Bounce rate: A high bounce rate suggests visitors aren’t finding what they expect. Regularly assess and improve content to lower bounce rates.
  • Click-through rate (CTR): Monitor your CTR to evaluate how well your title tags and meta descriptions are performing.
  • Keyword rankings: Track the performance of your target keywords. If search rankings decline, it may be time to revisit and optimize your content.

FAQ

What is the difference between off-page SEO and on-page SEO?

Off-page SEO focuses on optimizing signals that occur outside your website, such as building backlinks and establishing a social media presence. On-page SEO involves optimizing elements directly on your webpages, like content, meta tags, and internal links.

What is the difference between on-page SEO and technical SEO?

On-page SEO deals with optimizing individual web pages to improve their search engine rankings and user experience. Technical SEO involves optimizing the technical aspects of your entire website (e.g., site speed, crawlability, mobile-friendliness). The goal is to ensure it’s crawlable, indexable, and performs well from a technical standpoint.

How often should I perform on-page SEO (especially with AI Search updates)?

Due to constant algorithm updates and the rapid evolution of AI Search, on-page SEO requires ongoing maintenance, not a one-time fix. We recommend routine monitoring and minor updates at least monthly. Conduct a comprehensive content review (checking for LLM chunking, information gain, and E-E-A-T signals) conducted quarterly.

What are the key elements of modern on-page SEO?

Key elements of modern on-page SEO go beyond keywords. They include optimizing title tags and meta descriptions, ensuring strong information gain (original value), improving user experience, and critically, implementing LLM-friendly content structures (semantic chunking, clear headings, quotable definitions) to encourage AI citation.

How important is on-page SEO for overall website performance in the age of AI?

On-page SEO is integral to performance. A page that is poorly optimized will struggle to rank in traditional search, and it will be nearly **invisible to generative AI models**. Strong on-page signals (structure, E-E-A-T, and relevance) are the foundation for gaining traffic from both human searchers and AI summaries.

Are there tools available to assist with modern on-page SEO?

Yes, several tools can help you. Platforms like Semrush and others offer features for keyword research, content optimization based on topical authority, and technical checks to ensure your content is ready for both search engine crawlers and large language models.
